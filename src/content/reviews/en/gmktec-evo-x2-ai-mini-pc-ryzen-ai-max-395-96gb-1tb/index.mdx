## GMKtec EVO-X2 at a glance
The GMKtec EVO-X2 pairs AMD's Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with 96GB of LPDDR5X-8000 in a tightly packed 193mm chassis. That combination alone makes it unusual in the current overlap between mini PCs and AI-first workstations: you get a 16-core mobile-class processor, Radeon 8060S integrated graphics, and far more unified memory than most compact desktops offer. What the PASS A pack verifies cleanly is the hardware package itself, while real-world local-AI performance still depends on software support, thermal tuning, and the exact runtime you plan to use.

## AI Workloads and Compute Performance
At the heart of the EVO-X2 is the Ryzen AI Max+ 395, with 16 cores and 32 threads boosting up to 5.1 GHz. For local AI experimentation, the more interesting detail is not a vendor benchmark but the memory footprint: 96GB of LPDDR5X-8000 gives this system more headroom than the average mini PC for models, datasets, and creator workloads that quickly exhaust smaller shared-memory designs. PASS A does not include standardized benchmarks for this exact 96GB/1TB SKU, so the safer takeaway is capability headroom rather than a guaranteed token-per-second result. The Radeon 8060S and dual USB4 setup make it better suited to modern inference-oriented workflows than a typical office mini PC, but software maturity still matters.

## Memory Capacity and Thermals
To achieve this memory footprint and speed, the 96GB capacity is entirely soldered down. That removes any practical RAM upgrade path, but it also aligns with the high-bandwidth memory design this APU expects. Thermal management uses a triple-fan cooling system inside a 77mm-tall chassis, and that compact design has a tradeoff: user feedback points to meaningful fan noise and the possibility of black-screen instability under heavier sustained loads. In practice, this looks less like a silent office box and more like a compact workstation that may reward fan-curve tuning and realistic workload expectations.

## Expandability and Ports
The EVO-X2 embraces extensive connectivity standard to modern workstations. Display output spans dual USB4, HDMI 2.1, and DisplayPort 1.4, which safely drives up to four displays simultaneously—including up to 8K resolution at 60Hz. Between the SD 4.0 card reader, 2.5GbE networking, and Wi-Fi 7 on the backend, peripheral attachment handles vast data ingestion effortlessly. Storage is handled out-of-the-box by a 1TB PCIe 4.0 NVMe drive, with a second M.2 2280 slot available for future expansion.

## Verdict
The GMKtec EVO-X2 makes its case through packaging rather than hard benchmark proof: a Ryzen AI Max+ 395, 96GB of fast LPDDR5X memory, dual USB4, and compact dimensions that are far more desk-friendly than a full tower. The tradeoffs are real too, including soldered memory, premium pricing, and a thermal/noise profile that sounds closer to a compact workstation than a quiet office mini PC. If you want a small system with unusually large memory headroom for local AI experiments, creator workflows, and heavy multitasking, it is a very interesting configuration. If you want guaranteed model-throughput leadership without tuning, PASS A does not support making that promise.
